## Context

Epic: `EPIC-08`. This story builds the ping as the player sees it, using these
components from `docs/wiki/design/components.md`:

- **C-06, the reticle and target preview.** It aims at a detent, a machine or a
  doorway, with snapping, and shows an in-range or out-of-range state.
- **C-07, the ping marker.** It is drawn in the sender's hue. Your helper's ping
  gets an eye badge and shows through walls, on your screen only. Your own
  ping gets a white ring.
- **C-08, the helper-ping edge arrow.** It shows when your helper's ping is
  off-screen.
- **C-03, the player marker.** It shows an eye badge over your helper and a key
  badge over your partner.

The rules the display follows (`mechanics.md` §4.1):

- A ping has no text, so it stays outside "predefined text" (CA-1).
- "Your helper's ping" is computed from your own `SeatView.supplierId`. That is
  safe, because the helper's identity is already in your projection
  (`roles.md` §6).
- The client proposes a target and the server decides. A `PingRefused` shows as
  "did not land", and the button is ready again after the attempt floor, not
  after 10 s (G9).

**Which required gate would fail if this story's artifact broke:** `unit`. The
look is `D-1`.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** — Given candidates, the camera and the viewer's position, when
  `AimModel.pick` runs, then it returns the candidate nearest the screen centre
  within the snap radius, with `inRange` computed against `ping_range_studs`
  horizontally. A setting candidate carries its detent index.
- **AC-2** — Given `PingModel.describe(ping, viewer, now)`, when the sender is the
  viewer's `supplierId`, then the badge is `helper` and `alwaysOnTop` is true.
  When the sender is the viewer, the badge is `self`. For any other sender there
  is no badge and it is not always on top.
  *Control:* a model that badges every ping on the viewer's own machines must
  fail when a non-helper pings there.
- **AC-3** — Given `EdgeArrowModel.place`, when the helper's ping is on screen, it
  is hidden. When the ping is off-screen or behind the camera, it is placed on
  the safe-rect edge along the direction to the ping.
- **AC-4** — Given `ActionButtonModel.describe` for the ping button, when a
  `PingRefused` arrives, then the state is `not landed` and it is ready again
  after `channel_attempt_min_interval_seconds`. After an accepted ping, it
  cools for `ping_rate_limit_seconds`.
- **AC-5** — Given any ping output, when it is inspected, then it has no text
  field (CA-1).

## Contract

The models are pure, in `src/client/models/`, per `components.md`: `AimModel`,
`PingModel`, `EdgeArrowModel`, `ActionButtonModel` and `PlayerMarkerModel`. If
`HUD-002` ran first, `AimModel` is already built, and this story extends its
candidates. Pings are sent with
`ClientTransport.send("Ping", { kind, target, setting? })`.

**Oracle partition.**
- AC-2, AC-4 and AC-5 are **settled** by `mechanics.md` §4.1, `tuning.md` §3
  and `components.md`.
- AC-1 and AC-3 are **mechanical** geometry.
